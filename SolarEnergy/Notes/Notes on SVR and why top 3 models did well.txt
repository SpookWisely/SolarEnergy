--Why Are the Top 3 the Top 3?-- 
So first to look at when talking the top 3 model is what approach was taken for the measuring of the models success. Firstly this was done via a "Performance Matrix",
which contain 4 different aspects these in order were: MSE ( Mean Squared Error), MAE (Mean Absolute Error), RMSE (Root Mean Sqaure Error) and R2 (Coefficient Of Determination).

The first three metrics of the matrix (MSE,MAE & RMSE) follow the same logic when grading what is a better result. This is that the lowest of the values in these categories
is the better one in direct comparison between models. The reasoning behind this is that the lower the value represents a higher accuracy of a regression model in its predictions and
close they are to the true values that were inputted for the model to make its predictions.

Finally we have R2 (Coefficient Of Determination) which is related to the fitting of the model to the data. The score is given between a range of 0 to 1 with 0 being absolute worst and 1 being perfect.
So the logic is the closer the resuting R2 score is to 1 the better is. While looking at the other metrics R2 can have a much higher degree of importance as in if the other three metrics are better but
the R2 score very bad essentially disregards those more positive results for a model that has better fitting as it was more capable of capturing the data underlying pattersn and logic in its predictions.

For the other reasons as to why LSTM comes on top is mentioned [3] "The long short-term memory (LSTM) overcomes the error back-flow problems, by enforcing constant error flow through internal states of special units."
This backs up the point of the LSTM model being able to resolve the "Vanishing Gradient Problem" which is related to the issue that stems from multiplying the gradient
in the layers of neural network. This can lead few issues mainly poor performance and slow learning. So with this being addressed by these LSTM illustrates another reason
why LSTM ended up being the very best in the performance matrix results table.

An additional area to look over is the runner up models in seccond and third placement. In seccond place we have BLSTM which makes sense being that LSTM was the highest performer BLSTM shouldn't be far behind the main difference in BLSTM to BLSTM in relation to how this specific use case is 
that BLSTM [5]"Wraps another layer to make it bidirectional. For example, Bidirectional(LSTM(10)) will create a bidirectional LSTM layer.". Which extends the capabilities of a regular LSTM to be as the name
bidirectonal as in being able to go both ways in the layers. Also since the [6]Bidirectional nature of the model it allows the model to capture both the past and future context of the data its working and
as this data is time based is a rather large boon to its ability to make accurate predictions.

In third place We have GRU while this does have very close results to LSTM but albeit slightly worse. GRU does have less computational
cost when running. This is related to the fact that [4]"GRU is similar to an LSTM but has fewer parameters, making it faster to train and slightly less prone to overfitting.". With less parameters to go over
